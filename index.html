Why traditional satellite deforestation tracking fails in India — and how GeoClix solves cloud obstruction, seasonal false positives, and noisy classifications with fused orbital telemetry.

The Core Trilemma of Forest Surveillance in the Indian Subcontinent:

During the 4-month Southwest Monsoon (June–September), over 80% of India's forest cover is obscured by dense cloud decks, rendering standard optical satellites (Landsat, Sentinel-2) blind precisely when illegal logging and land grabbing peak. Furthermore, seasonal deciduous leaf-fall during dry months regularly produces massive false-positive alarms. GeoClix fuses Sentinel-1 C-band Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) with LandTrendr temporal segmentation to guarantee uninterrupted, 365-day verified detection.

1. Sentinel-2 Optical (10m)

Captures 13 multispectral bands every 5 days over India. Uses Normalized Difference Vegetation Index (NDVI) and Normalized Burn Ratio (NBR) to identify acute canopy degradation.

CYCLE: EVERY 5 DAYS • 10M GSD

2. Sentinel-1 SAR (Radar)

Synthetic Aperture Radar emits 5.405 GHz microwave pulses. Pierces heavy monsoon cloud decks, dense smoke, and darkness by measuring surface roughness and dielectric volume scattering.

ALL-WEATHER • MONSOON CLOUD PROOF

3. LandTrendr Temporal Analysis

Extracts 10-year pixel trajectory profiles from Landsat & Sentinel archives. Differentiates cyclical autumn leaf fall from permanent bulldozer clearance via temporal slope segmentation.

10-YEAR HISTORICAL ARCHIVE SEGMENTATION

4. Dual AI Verification & ESRGAN

Machine learning cross-validates alerts across GFW, FIRMS, and SAR feeds to discard noisy labels. Features optional ESRGAN 4x super-resolution upscaling (10m → 2.5m Ground Sampling Distance).
